DIME TIME — ORIGINAL GRAD-SCHOOL DECK VS. TODAY
July 2026

THE CORE IDEA — UNCHANGED

Original concept:
“Round up purchases to pay down debt. Get out of debt, one dime at a time.”

Today:
That same core concept is now a functioning fintech application available on the Apple App Store.

The product evolved. The central mission did not.

⸻

WHAT THE ORIGINAL DECK GOT RIGHT

1. Round-ups directed toward debt

The original presentation described using spare change from everyday purchases to help users reduce debt.

That is the core mechanism ultimately built into Dime Time.

2. Plaid as the bank-linking partner

The original deck identified Plaid as the infrastructure provider that could connect Dime Time with users’ financial accounts.

Years later, Plaid was integrated into the actual product.

3. Subscription-based monetization

The original business model anticipated recurring subscription revenue.

Dime Time’s current planned pricing begins at $2.99 per month for debt-payment round-ups.

4. The market thesis

The original deck cited approximately $14.3 trillion in U.S. household debt in 2020 and argued that the problem would continue growing.

By 2026, total U.S. household debt is materially higher. The underlying consumer problem became larger while Dime Time was being developed.

⸻

WHAT CHANGED

1. Financial-data sales were abandoned

Original plan:
Generate revenue by selling financial data through a data marketplace.

Current position:
Dime Time does not sell user financial data.

That model was abandoned because it conflicted with the company’s privacy principles, consumer trust requirements, platform expectations, and the long-term brand Dime Time intends to build.

2. The sweep-account architecture changed

Original plan:
Place accumulated round-ups into an investment or sweep account associated with JPMorgan Chase and potentially generate interest before transferring the funds.

Current architecture:
Dime Time maintains a round-up ledger and executes scheduled ACH transfers using Stripe infrastructure.

The behavioral concept survived, but the underlying payment mechanics became more practical and operationally realistic.

3. The development strategy changed completely

Original plan:
Raise approximately $500,000 in pre-seed capital, hire a development agency, and produce an MVP within approximately 90–180 days.

What happened:
The company did not receive the anticipated funding.

Several years later, the founder began building the product independently with AI-assisted development tools and third-party fintech infrastructure.

The result is:

* A live iOS application
* A functioning backend
* Real bank-linking infrastructure
* Production ACH money movement
* Approximately 35,000 lines of code
* More than 1,000 development commits
* A provisional patent filing
* Early registered users

The original plan depended on outside capital to begin.

The actual company was built through founder execution.

4. The team structure changed

Original deck:
Founder, developer, and legal support.

Today:
The product has primarily been built and directed by a solo founder using AI-assisted development, external APIs, professional services, and specialized infrastructure partners.

5. Pricing changed

Original plan:
Charge approximately $1 per month.

The original deck also criticized Qoins for charging $2.99 per month.

Today:
Dime Time’s planned entry-level debt round-up subscription is also $2.99 per month.

The market ultimately validated a higher subscription price than the original deck anticipated.

⸻

EVEN THE BRAND COLOR EVOLVED RATHER THAN DISAPPEARED

Original presentation color:
#B0B8FF — soft periwinkle

Current Dime Time purple:
#918EF4 — deeper and more saturated

The modern brand is recognizably connected to the original visual identity.

THE ONE-LINE STORY

The original deck presented an idea that never received the funding required to build it. The current company is that same idea, independently executed with AI-assisted development until it became a real fintech product.

Nothing fundamental changed.

Everything became real.
