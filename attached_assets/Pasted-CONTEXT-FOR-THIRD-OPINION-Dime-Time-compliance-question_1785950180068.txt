CONTEXT FOR THIRD OPINION — Dime Time compliance question (Aug 5, 2026)

Company: Dime Time (dime-time.com), live consumer debt-payoff app (iOS/Android/web),
solo non-technical founder, small user base, $2.99/mo subscription.

How the product works (traced from actual production code, not intended design):
1. User links checking account via Plaid; app computes spare-change round-ups.
2. Stripe ACH-debits the user's checking account (Stripe is ACH originator,
   statement descriptor "DIME TIME"). Funds settle in the company's Stripe balance.
3. Stripe auto-payouts sweep funds to the company's Mercury BUSINESS SAVINGS account
   (company-titled, ~3.25% APY). Subscription revenue lands in the SAME account —
   customer funds and company revenue are commingled.
4. A weekly engine (currently OFF in production) would, every Friday, ACH each
   user's accumulated balance from that Mercury savings account to the user's
   chosen creditor. No contracts exist with any creditor.
5. Per-user balances exist only in the app's own database ledger; there is no
   bank-level per-user recordkeeping and no automated reconciliation to the bank
   balance. Users skipped in a weekly run roll over indefinitely.

The founder's original revenue idea: keep the ~3.25% interest earned while user
funds sit in the savings account awaiting Friday disbursement.

An AI research analysis (2 rounds, 45+ sources incl. fincen.gov primary texts,
Mercury's ToS, SCOTUS case law) concluded the interest-retention model fails
three independent ways:

A. Mercury contract: Terms of Use §1.4 prohibits using the account "for any third
   parties"; Mercury support docs list "facilitating payments for third parties"
   as an account-closure ground. Mercury offers no FBO/custodial product.

B. Federal money transmission: FIN-2009-R004 held a bill-payment provider paying
   billers WITHOUT contracts IS a money transmitter ("there is no transaction
   other than a money transmission itself"). FinCEN Ruling 2004-4 exempted a
   debt-management company only because payments were ancillary to a substantive
   service (negotiating repayment plans/creditor concessions) — Dime Time only
   forwards money, doesn't negotiate. The payee-agent exemptions (2003-8,
   2008-R006, 2014-R009) all require creditor-side agreements Dime Time lacks.
   Conclusion: the accept→hold→forward flow likely = money transmission.

C. State licensing: 49 states license money transmission; agent-of-payee
   exemptions require appointment by the CREDITOR, not the consumer. Active
   multistate enforcement exists (Kraken 2024, Block/Cash App 2025).

D. Interest ownership: Phillips v. Washington Legal Foundation (SCOTUS 1998) —
   "interest follows principal"; interest on funds held for others belongs to
   the owners. PayPal/Venmo keep float only as licensed transmitters with
   segregated funds, permissible investments, and explicit user-agreement terms.

E. FDIC: company-titled account gives users no pass-through insurance
   (12 CFR 330.5/330.7); the app's unreconciled internal ledger is the Synapse
   failure pattern the FDIC's proposed custodial-records rule targets.

Recommendations the analysis produced:
1. Don't enable the weekly disbursement leg in production until reviewed by a
   fintech attorney (founder currently has none).
2. Segregate customer round-ups from subscription revenue; stop treating savings
   interest as revenue.
3. Move the payment leg to a bill-pay rail with creditor connectivity
   (Method Financial or Spinwheel — both sales-gated, unpublished pricing,
   estimated ~$0.50–$2/payment + possible monthly minimums). This also fixes
   the UX gap: big card issuers don't accept raw ACH, so today an admin would
   have to hand-enter creditor bank details per debt.
4. Long-term legitimate deposit revenue: FBO account at a sponsor bank via BaaS
   (Unit/Treasury Prime/etc.) where the BANK earns interest and pays the program
   a contractual revenue share.

QUESTIONS FOR YOU (third opinion):
1. Do you agree the interest-retention model is not viable as structured?
2. Do you agree with the FinCEN category analysis (FIN-2009-R004 non-contract
   branch applies; 2004-4 exemption does not)?
3. Is anything overstated or understated in the risk assessment?
4. Given a solo founder with no attorney and limited budget: what is the

5. How urgent is fixing the CURRENT state (round-up collection into a commingled

6. Are Method Financial / Spinwheel the right rails, or is there a cheaper
   alternative (e.g., returning funds to the user's own checking account)?
