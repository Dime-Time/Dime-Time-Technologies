CORE DECK (SLIDES 1-18)

01  COVER — From Sommelier to Fintech Founder
    Subtitle: How a Nontechnical Founder Built and Launched
    Dime Time. Speaker block: Tim Carlisle, Founder & CEO,
    Dime Time Technologies LLC, U.S. Navy Veteran, MBA •
    Graduate degree in Management Information Systems.
    Slogan: "Get out of debt, one dime at a time."

02  WHO I AM — A Nontraditional Path Into Technology
    Navy veteran, Las Vegas/Dallas hospitality, high-profile
    restaurant openings, sommelier, MBA + MIS graduate degree,
    founder of Dime Time. Core message: tech founders do not
    all begin as software engineers.

03  WHAT HOSPITALITY TAUGHT ME — Hospitality Was Founder Training
    Nine transferable skills (reading customers, pressure,
    clarity, trust, recovery, timing, consistency, standards,
    informal leadership) bridged to fintech trust requirements.

04  THE CLASSROOM FOUNDATION — The Idea Began as an Academic
    Exercise
    Entrepreneurship coursework frameworks + MIS education +
    early outsourced demo. Framing: the demo was a learning
    tool, not the product. No invented dates.

05  THE PROBLEM — Why Dime Time Needed to Exist
    Behavioral framing (intention vs. discipline) + verified
    market context: $18.8T household debt, $1.25T credit-card
    balances (NY Fed Q1 2026), 91% smartphone ownership (Pew
    2025). Explicit note: these are national statistics, not
    Dime Time metrics. Citations on-slide.

06  THE SOLUTION — Get Out of Debt, One Dime at a Time
    Six-step concept (connect → analyze → round up → accumulate
    → user controls destination/schedule → authorized ACH
    payments). Accuracy note: money moves on a user-approved
    schedule, not per purchase. Two real App Store screenshots.

07  FROM IDEA TO SYSTEM — A Fintech Product Is More Than an App
    Screen
    Seven-stage pipeline diagram (UX → bank connection →
    analysis → round-up calc → authorization → ACH → tracking/
    reconciliation) + the twelve capability areas required.

08  BUILDING WITHOUT AN ENGINEERING BACKGROUND
    Problem-first learning, AI-assisted development,
    decomposition, verification-over-trust, evidence habits.
    Core lesson: AI accelerates execution but does not replace
    founder judgment, testing, accountability.

09  THE TECHNOLOGY — The System Behind Dime Time
    Four stack cards: Front end (React/TypeScript/Capacitor),
    Back end (Express/Node, PostgreSQL, Drizzle), Financial
    infrastructure (Plaid approved products, Stripe ACH, Stripe
    Financial Connections under review, Mercury as business
    bank), Operations (Replit, Codemagic, App Store, Google
    Play prep). Disclaimer: partner names ≠ endorsements.
    Coinbase intentionally absent (planned only).

10  SAFETY BEFORE SCALE — Building Guardrails
    Twelve guardrail cards (master switch, ACH switch,
    allowlist, no auto-approval, first-transfer/daily/frequency
    limits, authorization evidence, duplicate protection,
    webhook verification, ledger, audit logging) + instant
    disable. Core lesson: safest successful test > uncontrolled
    launch.

11  THE HISTORICAL TIMELINE — From Academic Concept to Live
    Fintech Product
    2014-2025 idea/classroom era (no invented dates) →
    Aug 3, 2025 first commit → May 27, 2026 legal effective →
    Jun 29, 2026 App Store live → Jul 7, 2026 $1.00 controlled
    ACH test → Jul 21, 2026 v1.0.5 build 207 live + $0.99
    Mercury payout (loop proven) + Stripe FC submitted.

12  THE MOMENT THE MODEL BECAME REAL — One Dollar Changed the
    Project
    Flow: $1.00 ACH → Stripe → $0.99 payout → Mercury → loop
    proven. Status badge: PROVEN IN CONTROLLED LIVE TEST.
    Mandatory caveat: public transfers NOT enabled.

13  WHAT DID NOT HAPPEN OVERNIGHT — The Invisible Work Behind a
    Launch
    Twelve categories of invisible work (reviews, rejections,
    provider applications, approvals, security, legal, email,
    OAuth, screenshots, versioning, Android prep, queues).

14  SETBACKS AS SYSTEM FEEDBACK — Every Failure Became a Better
    Control
    Challenge/Response/Lesson table, five rows: duplicate-app
    rejection, email failures, real-money risk, OAuth return
    flow, Android permissions. Framed as learning, not blame.

15  WHAT AI DID AND DID NOT DO — Force Multiplier, Not Autopilot
    Two columns: 8 things AI helped with vs. 9 things that
    required the founder. Core lesson: the advantage is
    directing, questioning and verifying AI.

16  LESSONS FOR STUDENTS — What I Wish More Students Knew
    Ten numbered lessons + highlighted quote: "Your background
    does not disqualify you. It gives you a different set of
    raw materials."

17  WHAT COMES NEXT — The Next Chapter
    Banner: CURRENT ROADMAP — NOT COMPLETED MILESTONES.
    Google Play verification/signing/submission, Stripe FC
    approval → controlled public bank linking, Plaid
    Liabilities → debt import, controlled acquisition, metrics,
    $150,000 pre-seed raise (last item only). No promised dates.

18  CLOSING — WHAT WILL YOU BUILD?
    Closing quote, contact (dime-time.com / tim@dime-time.com),
    slogan. Open space for Q&A.
