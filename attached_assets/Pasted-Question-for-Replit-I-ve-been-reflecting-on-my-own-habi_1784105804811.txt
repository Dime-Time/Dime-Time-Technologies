Question for Replit

I’ve been reflecting on my own habits and personality traits.

Based on everything you know about me and Dime Time, what are my bad habits or personality traits that you think are holding me back?

Please be completely honest. Don’t just tell me positive things. Tell me where you think I get in my own way, where I waste time, where I could improve as a founder, and what behaviors you think would have the biggest impact if I changed them.

For context, here’s ChatGPT’s assessment.

ChatGPT’s Assessment

1. I sometimes try to advance too many major goals at the same time.

At any given time I’m often juggling:

* Building Dime Time
* Working 30–40+ hours at Delilah
* Wine study
* Family issues
* Financial stress
* Networking
* Career planning
* Personal relationships

While I have the capacity to do a lot, my attention becomes fragmented. Dime Time is clearly my long-term priority, yet many other urgent matters compete for my energy.

⸻

2. I spend a lot of time trying to solve other people’s problems.

Examples include:

* Don
* Family conflicts
* Coworkers
* Friends asking for money or career advice

I’ve noticed that people often come to me for help, introductions, or emotional support, but that energy isn’t always returned.

I’ve started setting healthier boundaries, and continuing to do that would likely free up more time and mental energy for Dime Time.

⸻

3. I naturally think several moves ahead.

This is a strength, but it can also become a distraction.

While Dime Time is still in its early commercialization stage, I’ve already spent significant time thinking about:

* Large fundraising rounds
* International expansion
* Becoming a bank
* Amsterdam
* Dubai headquarters
* Luxury purchases
* Long-term scaling

There’s nothing wrong with having a vision, but execution today matters more than planning years into the future.

⸻

4. Cash-flow stress creates decision fatigue.

I’ve spent months balancing:

* Rent
* Car expenses
* Plaid invoices
* Replit costs
* Business expenses

Constant financial pressure consumes mental bandwidth that could otherwise be invested into product development and company growth.

⸻

5. I sometimes underestimate the value of letting relationships develop naturally.

Tonight’s conversation with Vipin is a good example.

Instead of pitching Dime Time immediately, I:

* Introduced myself.
* Thanked him.
* Talked about living and working in the Design District.
* Built rapport.

That approach likely creates a much stronger long-term relationship than trying to turn every interaction into a business opportunity.

⸻

6. I hold myself to a very high standard and sometimes become frustrated when others don’t.

I frequently notice mistakes, inefficiencies, or poor decision-making from coworkers or management.

That attention to detail is valuable, but spending too much energy thinking about what others should be doing can distract me from making progress toward my own goals.

What ChatGPT thinks I’m doing well

According to ChatGPT:

* I’m persistent.
* I adapt when setbacks happen.
* I network comfortably with influential people.
* I continue learning across multiple disciplines.
* I consistently show up and perform when opportunities arise.

Biggest Habit to Change

The single biggest recommendation was:

Protect my time and attention as aggressively as I protect my money.

Every hour spent worrying about workplace drama, other people’s problems, or objectives that aren’t immediately important is an hour not invested in the one thing most likely to change my life—building Dime Time into a successful company.
