## Key Facts (bullets w/ URLs)

**Gap A — Money transmitter exposure & exemptions**
- Every US state except Montana regulates money transmission under a licensing regime; laws generally require a license in EACH state where you have customers for "receiving money for transmission or otherwise facilitating payments on behalf of others." A hold-and-forward model like Dime Time (receiving consumer ACH funds, holding, then remitting to a creditor) squarely fits "money transmission." — https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2024/2024-08-20-us-states-adopt-model-money-transmission-act-but-harmonization-remains-elusive
- The **agent-of-the-payee exemption** exempts a licensee-free party who "receives money from a person for payment for goods or services to a third party" — but it requires the recipient be a genuine appointed agent of the PAYEE (the creditor), with a contract making payment to the agent satisfy the payer's obligation to the payee. Dime Time is appointed by the *consumer (payer)*, not the creditor — so it likely does NOT qualify absent creditor-side agency agreements. — CSBS map: https://www.csbs.org/agent-payee-exemption-map ; CA DFPI bill-payment opinion (agent must act as the *payee's* agent): https://dfpi.ca.gov/rules-enforcement/laws-and-regulations/opinion-letters-by-law-subject/agent-of-payee-exemption-bill-payment/
- As of Jan 2025, **26 states** had adopted the CSBS Model Money Transmission Modernization Act (MMTMA) in whole/part; MA became the 49th state to regulate domestic money transmission (effective 1/1/2026). MMTMA broadly includes an agent-of-a-payee exemption, but adoption is fractured — states deviate on scope. — https://www.stinson.com/newsroom-publications-massachusetts-adopts-the-model-money-transmission-modernization-act-joining-25-other-states ; https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2024/2024-08-20-us-states-adopt-model-money-transmission-act-but-harmonization-remains-elusive
- **Enforcement — multi-state coordinated action (Kraken/Payward):** Oregon DFR issued a Cease & Desist/consent order (Case MT-24-0052, March 27, 2024) and Texas (Oct 28, 2024), Ohio, Arkansas, and Maine took parallel actions against Payward/Kraken for money-transmission licensing deficiencies — illustrating how states coordinate against unlicensed/deficient transmitters. — https://dfr.oregon.gov/news/news2024/Pages/20240327-cryptocurrency-settlement.aspx ; https://www.dob.texas.gov/sites/default/files/files/news/press-releases/2024/10-28-24pr.pdf ; https://dam.assets.ohio.gov/image/upload/com.ohio.gov/FIIN/Enforcements/fiin_Enf2024_Payward_Ventures.pdf
- **Block/Cash App multistate consent order (Jan 13, 2025 CSBS-coordinated; NY DFS separate $40M order April 10, 2025)** — shows regulators pursue even licensed transmitters for BSA/AML and program deficiencies; a small fintech holding consumer funds without licenses faces cease-and-desist, penalties, and forced restructuring. — https://www.csbs.org/sites/default/files/other-files/Block_Settlement_and_Consent_OrderFinal_1.13.2025-order%20only.pdf ; https://www.dfs.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2025/04/ea20250410-block.pdf
- **FinCEN federal layer:** Money transmitters must register federally as MSBs; FinCEN maintains an enforcement list for failure to register (e.g., Paxful consent order 2025-02). Note: a DOJ April 2025 "Ending Regulation by Prosecution" memo said DOJ won't bring §1960 unlicensed-transmission charges absent willfulness — but this does NOT relieve state civil licensing obligations. — https://www.fincen.gov/enforcement-actions-failure-register-money-services-business ; https://www.gibsondunn.com/2025-year-end-developments-in-anti-money-laundering/

**Gap B — Programs legally keeping yield on customer float**
- **PayPal** user agreement (updated 6/29/2026): "PayPal owns the interest or other earnings on these investments. However... you do not have any ownership interest (either legal or beneficial) in these investments." Balances are pooled, invested in liquid investments per state money-transmitter laws, held apart from corporate funds, not used for operating expenses. This is the canonical "program keeps the float yield" structure. — https://www.paypal.com/us/legalhub/paypal/useragreement-full
- **Venmo** user agreement (eff. 8/24/2026): near-identical language — "You will not receive any interest or other return on the funds held with us. We own the interest or other earnings on these investments." — https://venmo.com/legal/us-user-agreement/
- **Structural lesson:** Both keep yield by (1) being licensed money transmitters, (2) explicit user-agreement disclosure that the user gets no interest and no beneficial ownership of investments, (3) pooling/segregating from corporate funds, (4) investing only in statutorily "permissible investments."
- **Robinhood** High-Yield Cash Program uses an IntraFi network deposit sweep — it PASSES an APY to the customer (opposite structure); shows the alternative where yield goes to the user. — https://cdn.robinhood.com/assets/robinhood/legal/IntraFi%20Network%20Deposit%20Sweep%20Program%20Agreement.pdf
- **Wise Interest** also passes yield to the customer (opt-in, passthrough FDIC via program bank) — again the user-gets-yield model, contrasting PayPal/Venmo. — https://wise.com/us/interest/
- **BaaS providers** explicitly market "interest revenue" as a program revenue stream: Unit's guide frames earning "a share of the economics generated by those deposits" from customer balances held at a partner bank via FBO accounts. Treasury Prime and Stripe Treasury structure funds through FBO / financial accounts. — https://www.unit.co/guides/how-high-interest-rates-can-drive-revenue-in-tech ; https://www.treasuryprime.com/blog/fbo-account ; https://docs.stripe.com/treasury/connect/v2/account-management/financial-accounts

## Notable Claims Requiring Cross-Reference
- Whether Dime Time can qualify for agent-of-payee: hinges on whether it can obtain agency appointment from CREDITORS (payees), not just consumers. The CA DFPI opinion strongly implies consumer-appointed bill-pay does NOT qualify — verify against each target state's statute individually.
- PayPal/Venmo keep yield while LICENSED as money transmitters and investing only in "permissible investments." A Mercury *company savings account* is likely NOT a statutorily permissible investment vehicle and commingles risk — this materially distinguishes Dime Time from PayPal. Needs legal confirmation.
- The "up to 7 days hold" is central: many state MT statutes and the agent-of-payee exemption have no explicit safe-harbor hold period; holding period alone rarely creates/removes MT status — the receipt-and-forward activity does. Cross-check any per-state timing carve-outs.
- Exact current count of states with agent-of-payee exemption (CSBS map is dated 2019; MMTMA adoption changed the landscape) — needs the live CSBS map state-by-state pull.
- DOJ non-prosecution memo (Gibson Dunn) is federal criminal only — does not touch state civil licensing; do not over-rely on it.

## Gaps & Unanswered Questions
- No single authoritative, current (2025-26) state-by-state list of which states have agent-of-payee AND receive-and-forward exemptions with their exact conditions — the CSBS map is the best pointer but must be pulled per-state.
- No enforcement example found of a *small* debt-payoff/round-up fintech specifically penalized (examples are crypto/large players); the risk is analogized, not directly precedented.
- Whether holding funds in a company Mercury savings account (vs. statutory permissible investments) independently defeats the PayPal-style "we own the interest" defense — needs securities/MT counsel.
- No direct precedent found of a BaaS program contractually keeping FBO interest AND disclosing it in a consumer agreement the way PayPal does (marketing pages assert it's possible, but no sample consumer disclosure retrieved).
