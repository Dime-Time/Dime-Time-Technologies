## Key Facts

- **Default legal rule: "interest follows principal."** The U.S. Supreme Court held that interest earned on client funds held by a third party is the **private property of the client (the fund owner), not the custodian**. The common-law maxim "interest shall follow the principal, as the shadow the body" (Beckford v. Tobin, 1749) is "firmly embedded in the common law of the various States." — Phillips v. Washington Legal Foundation, 524 U.S. 156 (1998), https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/96-1578.ZO.html  ⇒ Conservative implication: absent a clear, enforceable contractual reassignment, interest on pooled customer round-ups presumptively belongs to the users, not Dime Time.
- **CFPB acknowledges float revenue is possible only "subject to applicable law."** "Companies offering stored value services can also make money from interest generated by investing funds their customers store on the app, subject to applicable law." — CFPB Issue Spotlight, https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/research-reports/issue-spotlight-analysis-of-deposit-insurance-coverage-on-funds-stored-through-payment-apps/full-report/ (JUN 01, 2023 — **>18 months old, flag**). The report frames uninsured/stored-funds practices and unclear ToS as consumer-risk issues.
- **State money-transmitter law imposes a statutory trust over customer funds and their proceeds.** Permissible-investment statutes deem the funds — "even if commingled with other assets of the licensee… held in trust for the benefit of the purchasers and holders of the licensee's outstanding… stored value obligations." — NC Gen. Stat. § 53-208.48 (2025), https://law.justia.com/codes/north-carolina/chapter-53/article-16b/section-53-208-48/ (parallel statutes: FL §560.210, HI §489D-8, WA RCW 19.230.210, VA Ch. 19.1). ⇒ Collecting/holding/transmitting consumer money likely triggers **state money-transmitter licensing**, and the trust framing reinforces that funds (and arguably proceeds) are held for customers.
- **FDIC pass-through insurance requires custodial titling + beneficial-owner recordkeeping — not just a ToS statement.** Two prongs, both required: (1) the bank's deposit-account records disclose the custodial/fiduciary nature of the account; (2) records identify each beneficial owner and their amount (kept by bank or custodian). — FDIC, 12 C.F.R. §§ 330.5 & 330.7, https://www.fdic.gov/financial-institution-employees-guide-deposit-insurance/pass-through-deposit-insurance-coverage
- **Dime Time's current structure likely defeats pass-through insurance.** A plain business **savings account in the company's own name (Mercury)** — not an FBO/custodial-titled account with a per-user ledger — means funds sit as the company's own deposit; if the bank fails, customers are unlikely to get pass-through coverage, and holding pooled customer money as company funds raises the money-transmitter/trust issues above. "Pass-through is a recordkeeping achievement, not a contractual promise. You do not get coverage because your terms of service say the word 'insured.'" — Montague Law, https://montague.law/blog/fdic-pass-through-fbo-fintech-disclosure/
- **Marketing/disclosing FDIC coverage is separately regulated (12 C.F.R. Part 328, §328.102).** A non-bank may not imply it is itself insured; must disclose coverage applies only to bank failure and that pass-through conditions apply. Defensible language is conditional ("may be eligible… provided conditions are satisfied, no guarantee"). — Montague Law (same URL). ⇒ Even if Dime Time fixes structure, "your funds are FDIC insured" claims are non-compliant.
- **Disclosure vs. consent / UDAAP risk on retained interest.** CFPB UDAAP enforcement (late-2022 consent order against a major bank) turned on a "disconnect between the Bank's disclosures and its product implementation"; regulators expect disclosures to match actual practice, and buried ToS terms that harm consumers invite unfairness/abusiveness findings. — Wolters Kluwer / BankingExchange (Feb 14, 2023), https://www.wolterskluwer.com/en/expert-insights/a-thoughtful-approach-to-aligning-bank-deposit-and-disclosure-activities ⇒ Conservative posture: a boilerplate ToS line is weak; **affirmative, conspicuous, specific consent** to the company retaining interest (with amount/rate context) is the defensible standard, and retaining interest silently while marketing "we help you pay off debt" risks a deception/abusiveness theory.
- **State escrow/consumer-funds statutes frequently mandate interest goes to the consumer or a public fund, not the holder.** E.g., Maryland Com. Law §12-1026, Ohio R.C. §3953.231, Oregon ORS 696.578 govern treatment of interest/earnings on held consumer funds. — https://law.justia.com/codes/maryland/commercial-law/title-12/subtitle-10/section-12-1026/ ; https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_696.578 ⇒ Analogous state regimes assume the holder is NOT the default owner of interest.

## Gaps & Unanswered Questions
- No source found in which a US regulator squarely blesses a fintech **keeping** float interest on consumer balances as revenue; the affirmative-consent bar is inferred, not stated as a bright-line rule. This absence itself supports the conservative "not allowed unless documented" posture.
- No definitive 50-state answer on whether round-up collection triggers MTL, or whether a bank-partner/FBO structure would exempt Dime Time.
- No located example of a debt-payoff app's ToS clause specifically retaining interest — comparable-language benchmarking is a gap.
- Unclear how Truth in Savings (Reg DD / 12 C.F.R. 1030) would apply if Dime Time ever markets the "3.25% APY" — Reg DD governs interest-bearing account disclosures and could be implicated if users are led to expect yield.
- Current federal-agency posture (2025) on payment-app stored funds under the new CFPB leadership is uncertain.
