## Key Facts

- **The "keep the interest on pooled customer balances" model is legally viable ONLY under a specific structure — it is the PayPal/Venmo model, and it hinges on money-transmitter compliance, not just an account name.** PayPal's own statement: funds are "held separate from PayPal's corporate funds… PayPal owns the interest on the funds. PayPal complies with applicable state money transmitter laws pertaining to the funds." (marketplace.org, 2018 — STALE but corroborated by current CFPB doc below)
- **CFPB confirms this is a recognized revenue model but conditions it on law:** "Companies offering stored value services can also make money from interest generated by investing funds their customers store on the app, *subject to applicable law*." The report explicitly ties this to state **money transmitter** licensing (funds "invested in liquid investments in accordance with state money transmitter laws"). (consumerfinance.gov Issue Spotlight, Jun 1, 2023)
- **"FBO" is a naming convention, not a legal structure.** What governs is the underlying account agreement — the account can be a demand, escrow, trust, custody, or savings account; adding "FBO" only signals a split between legal and beneficial title. Whether pass-through FDIC insurance applies depends on the FDIC's pass-through requirements (per-beneficiary records). (troutmanfinancialservices.com, Sep 2024)
- **Concrete debt-payoff-app precedent — Qoins:** Court filings show Qoins (an ACH round-up debt-payoff app, structurally near-identical to Dime Time) set up THREE account types at its partner bank (Atlantic Capital Bank → SouthState): (1) **Custodial Accounts** titled "for the benefit of Customers" — contractually barred from use in Qoins's operations; (2) an **Operating Account**; and (3) a **Reserve Account.** The bank explicitly disclaimed possession of customer funds; disputes/charge-offs on Qoins's negative *operating* balances triggered litigation. (SouthState Bank v. Qoins, N.D. Ga., decided Mar 1, 2024)
- **Pass-through FDIC insurance requires per-beneficiary recordkeeping and is only adjudicated after a bank failure** — it "is extremely difficult to verify before a bank… fails," and the FDIC as receiver makes the final call. A pooled account without adequate sub-ledger records can be frozen at only $250k total. (consumerfinance.gov, 2023; klaros.com, 2024)
- **Synapse collapse is the cautionary tale for pooled/FBO customer funds:** ~$65–95M (trustee cited $85M) shortfall between what partner banks held and what fintech end-users were owed; hundreds of thousands lost access for months. Root cause was reliance on a middleware's ledger, NOT the "FBO" label itself. (troutmanfinancialservices.com; cnbc.com Jun 7, 2024; techcrunch.com Aug 22, 2024)
- **Regulators are actively tightening rules on exactly this structure.** FDIC proposed the "Recordkeeping for Custodial Accounts" rule (a.k.a. "FBO Rule"/"Synapse Rule") on Sep 17, 2024 (12 CFR Part 375), requiring banks holding custodial/FBO funds with transactional features to maintain accurate per-beneficiary records. Comment period was extended into late 2024. (fdic.gov press release Sep 17, 2024; federalregister.gov)
- **PayPal/Venmo's current live structure:** eligible balances are swept into named **Program Banks (Goldman Sachs Bank USA, Wells Fargo, JPMorgan Chase)** for pass-through FDIC insurance — but only when the customer triggers specific activities (debit card, direct deposit, crypto). "PayPal is not a bank, does not take deposits and is not FDIC insured." (paypal.com legal hub, updated May 19, 2026)
- **Acorns is a NON-comparable structure** (useful contrast): Round-Ups accumulate at the linked external checking account and are only pulled once they reach ≥$5, then invested via SEC-registered adviser/broker-dealer (custody by the broker-dealer). Acorns does NOT pool spare change in a savings account to earn float — funds transit into securities. (support.acorns.com; acorns.com disclosures)
- **Qapital** uses an external linked "Funding Account" (checking) to trigger goals; consumer-facing docs do not disclose keeping float, and its savings/goal balances sit at a partner bank. (help.qapital.com, Mar 2025)

## Notable Claims Requiring Cross-Reference
- PayPal's "PayPal owns the interest" language comes from a 2018 article (STALE). The *principle* is corroborated by the 2023 CFPB report and PayPal's current user agreements, but the exact contractual clause should be re-verified in PayPal's current Balance Terms/User Agreement before relying on it as a template.
- Whether keeping float requires a state MTL for Dime Time specifically depends on whether Dime Time itself is "in the funds flow." Sources establish MTL is the relevant regime for holding/investing customer funds, but the precise trigger (holding vs. mere agent-of-payee routing) is not resolved in these sources and needs dedicated MTL analysis.
- Qoins court doc describes the *account architecture* but does not state who earned interest on the Qoins custodial accounts — do not assume Qoins kept it.
- FDIC custodial-accounts rule's final status/effective date post-2024 not confirmed in fetched sources (only proposal + comment extension) — verify whether finalized.

**Bottom-line for the conservative analysis:** The only well-documented model where a fintech *keeps* interest on pooled customer balances is the licensed money-transmitter model (PayPal/Venmo), and even there it is (a) contractually disclosed to users, (b) funds segregated from corporate funds, and (c) done "in accordance with state money transmitter laws." The comparable debt-payoff app (Qoins) used custodial "FBO" accounts contractually walled off from operations. No source supports retaining float on a pooled Mercury savings account without (i) an MTL analysis, (ii) explicit user-agreement disclosure, and (iii) per-beneficiary recordkeeping for pass-through FDIC — and the Synapse/FDIC-rule material shows regulators are actively penalizing exactly the loose pooled-FBO structure Dime Time currently describes. Under the "nothing is allowed unless documented" standard, keeping the interest is NOT documented as permissible on the current architecture.
